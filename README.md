# PayLens

**Know the payment risk before you sign the deal.**

PayLens is a pre-contract payment-risk tool designed to help small businesses understand whether they can afford to take on a large customer contract.

## The Problem

Winning a large customer can look like great news for a small business. However, the business may need to pay staff, suppliers and operating costs before the customer actually pays them.

If the customer takes 60 or 90 days to pay, a profitable contract can still create serious cash-flow pressure.

PayLens helps answer:

**Can your small business afford to take this deal?**

## What PayLens Does

PayLens allows a small business owner to:

1. Search for a prospective customer.
2. View the customer's historical payment behaviour.
3. See their payment-delay risk.
4. Enter details about a proposed contract.
5. See how much exposure the contract could create.
6. Understand why the deal may be risky.
7. Change the upfront payment and payment terms.
8. See how restructuring the deal changes the exposure.

The main idea behind PayLens is:

**Customer Risk ≠ Contract Risk**

The same customer can create very different levels of risk depending on the size of the contract, the business's available cash and the structure of the deal.

## Example

A small business is considering a:

* $120,000 contract
* $45,000 available cash
* $25,000 monthly operating costs
* 60-day payment term
* 0% upfront payment

PayLens first assesses the customer's payment behaviour and then combines this with the proposed deal.

The user can then test a different contract structure, such as:

* 30% upfront payment
* 30-day payment term

PayLens recalculates the exposure so the user can compare the original and revised deal.

**Same customer. Different deal structure. Lower exposure.**

## Current Prototype

The current version focuses on building the complete PayLens website and user experience.

Customer search, customer payment history and payment-delay risk currently use **demo data**.

The contract exposure calculator uses a transparent prototype calculation based on:

* Customer payment-delay risk
* Contract value
* Available cash
* Monthly operating costs
* Payment terms
* Upfront payment

Real Australian Government payment data and the final machine-learning model will be integrated later.
